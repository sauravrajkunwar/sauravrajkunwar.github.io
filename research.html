Research & Projects

My research uses applied microeconomics, causal inference, structural modeling, productivity measurement, and spatial data to study firm and producer performance, innovation, resource allocation, environmental valuation, climate risk, and decision-making under uncertainty.

Working Paper in Progress

Index Investing and Productivity of U.S. Firms

This project studies how index investing relates to productivity outcomes among U.S. firms. I use difference-in-differences and triple-difference designs to examine how capital-market incentives and firm behavior are associated with changes in productivity and performance over time.

The broader goal is to understand how changes in ownership structure and financial incentives translate into real economic outcomes when randomized experiments are not available.

Working Paper

Agricultural TFP, Public R&D, and Patents

This project examines long-run productivity convergence across U.S. states and the role of public research investment and innovation in explaining differences in productivity growth.

The analysis links public R&D, patent activity, and productivity performance to study how innovation contributes to persistent differences in economic outcomes over time.

Job Market Paper

Pricing Agricultural Environmental Services Under Policy-Consistent Normalizations

This paper studies how conservation programs value environmental services when policy objectives differ. Practice-based programs compensate farmers for adopting specific management practices, while outcome-based programs pay for measured environmental improvements. I develop a duality-based framework to compare the private costs and incentive implications of these designs.

Using Illinois corn production data, the framework estimates shadow prices for carbon sequestration, greenhouse gas abatement, and erosion control. The analysis shows how policy normalization changes the economic value assigned to environmental outcomes and provides a consistent basis for designing incentives aligned with measurable goals.

Research in Progress

Weather Risk, Resource Misallocation, and Smallholder Productivity

This project combines household panel data with satellite-based climate measures to study how major weather shocks affect productivity and resource allocation among smallholder producers.

I use production-function estimation, productivity decomposition, and event-study methods to examine how external shocks affect producer performance and whether resulting misallocation persists over time.

Grant-Supported Research

Economic Value of Irrigation Information for Texas High Plains Cotton

This project develops an expected-value-of-information framework to quantify the economic value of improved irrigation information for producer decision-making.

The research combines economic modeling with a randomized on-farm experiment to evaluate how information affects management choices, resource allocation, and economic outcomes under uncertainty.

Published Article

Carbon Services from Cover-Crop Adoption

This study estimates the economic value of carbon services from cover-crop adoption, including carbon sequestration and greenhouse gas reductions. Using a data envelopment analysis framework and the dual representation of production technology, we compare environmental service values across cover-crop and non-cover-crop fields.

The paper finds carbon sequestration and greenhouse gas emission reductions from cover-crop fields valued at USD 17.88 and USD 9.42 per acre, respectively, and compares those values with incentive payments for sustainable practice adoption.
